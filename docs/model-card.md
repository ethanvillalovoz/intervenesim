# InterveneSim-Value model card

## Counterfactual value gate

The primary v0.3 model is a compact two-layer PyTorch MLP. Its inputs are the current
35-dimensional task-conditioned state, a causal one-step observation delta, and the
autonomous policy action. It predicts the probability that scripted expert takeover will
turn an autonomous failure into success from the exact same saved simulator state.

The model is trained on counterfactual states generated by policy seeds 27, 127, and 227.
The primary evaluation uses unseen policy seeds 327 and 427. Complete episodes remain in a
single split. Score thresholds are chosen only from training-policy episodes.

On 1,517 held-out-policy candidates, the value gate reaches AUROC 0.817, average precision
0.753, and Brier score 0.180. The released failure-risk and ensemble-uncertainty comparators
reach AUROC 0.706 and 0.703 respectively.

## Budget-aware deployment

At the 25% target budget, the value gate reaches 66.2% task success at 23.3% realized
intervention and 91.1% useful-request precision. The risk gate reaches 60.4% success at
24.6% intervention and 62.7% precision. Full frontiers, rather than a single operating
point, are frozen in `results/intervenesim-value`.

## Visual value probe

The secondary probe uses a frozen torchvision ResNet-18 with ImageNet-1K V1 weights. A
learned MLP head combines 512 visual features with robot state, control progress, and task
identity. Object and goal coordinates are withheld. The head trains only on front-view
images from policy seed 27 and evaluates on seed 327.

Front-view AUROC is 0.712. Zero-shot agent-view AUROC is 0.696, but recall at threshold 0.5
drops from 80.6% to 1.9%. The representation retains some ranking signal across viewpoints;
the score is not calibrated for camera transfer. This is not an end-to-end visual policy.

## Earlier v0.2 models

The task- and phase-routed behavior-cloned policy and temporal failure-risk gate from
InterveneSim-X remain documented in the v0.2 technical report. Recovery behavior cloning
reaches 61.4% disturbed success versus 38.6% for equal-count clean labels.

## Limitations

These models are research artifacts for scripted simulation. The value label depends on
the exact candidate grid, disturbance process, takeover semantics, simulator, and expert.
The models are not safety systems, real-robot policies, human-value estimators, or evidence
of sim-to-real transfer.
